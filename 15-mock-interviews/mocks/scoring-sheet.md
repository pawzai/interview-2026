# Scoring sheet

One sheet per round. Print it, or copy the block below into a document per loop.

Score from a **recording**, not from memory. Scoring immediately after a round is worse than scoring an hour later from the audio, because the feeling of the round contaminates the score (Q11).

---

## The four dimensions

Every round is scored on the same four, because these are what interviewers are actually recording (Q7).

| | 1 - Below bar | 2 - Senior | 3 - Principal | 4 - Exceptional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Depth** | Surface answer, no mechanism | Correct mechanism when asked | Volunteered the mechanism, held up under three "why"s | Went past the interviewer's own depth, and knew where the edge was |
| **Trade-offs** | Assertions with no cost named | Named a cost when prompted | Every recommendation carried a cost, an alternative and a condition | Reframed the decision so the trade-off was quantified |
| **Production judgement** | Textbook only | One relevant anecdote | Specific operational detail - a number, a failure, a thing that surprised them | Changed a class of problem, not an instance |
| **Communication** | Interviewer had to work to follow | Clear, but unstructured | Structure stated up front and kept, checkpointed, collaborative | Interviewer could repeat the whole answer back afterwards |

A round scoring 3 across the board is a hire at principal. Two 2s with no 4s is usually a hire at the level below - which is the most common bad outcome (Q1, Q5).

## The behavioural markers worth counting

These are objective, audible on a recording, and they move week over week - which makes them better practice targets than the four scores.

**Count the positives.** Each occurrence is worth a tally:

- Stated the structure before answering ("there are three parts to this").
- Gave a number without being asked.
- Named an alternative you rejected, and why.
- Named the cost of your own recommendation.
- Checkpointed - "shall I go deeper here or move on?"
- Corrected yourself, cleanly and briefly.
- Said "I do not know" with a boundary and a way to find out.
- Asked a clarifying question that changed your answer.

**Count the negatives.** These are the ones that show up on a recording and never in memory:

- Filler openings: "so basically", "as I mentioned", "at the end of the day". They cluster exactly where you are unsure.
- Answering a question you were not asked.
- Overran a short question past two minutes (Q203).
- Trailed off without landing the answer.
- Repeated an anecdote already used in this loop.
- Argued a point past sixty seconds (Q194).
- Apologized more than once for the same thing.

## Loop summary

Fill this in once, after all five rounds.

```
Loop:                                                Date:

Round scores (D / T / P / C):
  1.
  2.
  3.
  4.
  5.

Which round degraded most, and was it fatigue or content?
Did any story get used twice?
Did any two rounds contradict each other?
Would a panel reading these five sets of notes hire at principal, at senior, or not at all?
The single highest-yield thing to change before the next loop:
```

**The last question is the one that matters.** A loop that produces five scores and no change is a rehearsal you have already had.
